# Starter provenance

`best/` originates from vxvware/saw-best-matrix, commit
8cdf530f5461eacabebb0fb3294e12b829115031 (origin/exact-census).
The C census has local hardening: supported-order bounds, fail-closed solution
buffer limits, output-file error checks, and conservative spectral margins.
It is replayed as a computation, not advertised as a formal proof. The SAW
files are retained as research kernels and excluded from the trusted verifier.

`good/good_c.c` is the exact census of circulant good matrices from vxvware/saw-best-matrix, in the build that reran the n=65 and n=67 censuses after the wide-word defects recorded as finding 522 of that repository's optimization log; it is the source that runs on bos-game and archdesktop in October 2026. It is replayed as a computation with the wide build flags it was validated under, and only at odd orders 3 to 43, where a replay takes under a minute on two threads. Before it was pinned, its class sets under equiv-v1 were checked equal to the Python enumerator at orders 3 to 15, to the stored saw-best-matrix censuses at orders 17 to 45, and to the published counts of Bright, Đoković, Kotsireas and Ganesh (arXiv:1811.05094) at every order divisible by 3 up to 45. Its environment variables only tune performance; the verifier strips them anyway. Beyond order 43 the same source, built with the same wide flags, is the registered-replay engine good-census-c-v1: a maintainer runs it to completion offline, and the board re-derives the classes from its raw rows.

`williamson/williamson_c.c` is a new exact census of Williamson quadruples written for this board in October 2026: a meet in the middle over the symmetric sequences that survive the spectral ceiling, bucketed by the four-square decomposition of 4n, with an exact autocorrelation match and a recheck of every emitted quadruple. It is replayed at odd orders 3 to 37. Its class sets under equiv-v1 equal the classification of Holzmann, Kharaghani and Tayfeh-Rezaie (2008) at every order from 3 to 35, including the empty order 35.

`williamson/wc_compress.c` and `williamson/wc_lift.c` are the Williamson compression pipeline (engine id williamson-compress-v1), written 2026-11-12 for the orders the direct census cannot reach and registered offline through baselines/census_replays.json. For odd n = d m, wc_compress enumerates the 2^((n-1)/2) symmetric sequences with x_0 = +1, keeps those whose row sum fits a decomposition of 4n into four odd squares and whose PSD never exceeds 4n, compresses each survivor to length m (entry j is the sum over the coset j + k m), and matches the realized compressions into quadruples per decomposition on the length-m PAF condition with the PSD pair filter; one instance per orbit of the multipliers of Z_m is written, which loses nothing because every unit of Z_m lifts to a unit of Z_n. wc_lift enumerates, for each instance, every full sequence with that compression (coset by coset, pruned by the remaining capacity), keeps those under the PSD ceiling, and matches the four lists on the exact PAF sum with the sharded pair table of williamson_c.c; every emitted quadruple is rechecked from its rows. The pipeline reproduces the direct census exactly at 25, 35 and 45, with and without the multiplier reduction, and the published classifications at 49, 51, 55 and 57 (Holzmann, Kharaghani and Tayfeh-Rezaie) and 63 (Bright, Kotsireas and Ganesh).

`williamson/wc_lift5.c` (engine id williamson-compress-v2, with the same wc_compress and the original lifter) is the faster lifter written 2026-11-13: it also compresses every lifted sequence by m, to length d, whose PAF the four sequences must still cancel, groups each list by that length-d PAF vector and enumerates only the pairs of groups that some pair of groups on the other side can meet; chooses, when the four lists are distinct, the meet-in-the-middle split with the least group-pair work; tests the PSD pair ceiling on 16-lane vectors, eight sequences per tile; indexes the pair table by key buckets instead of sorting it; lifts the cosets of one compressed sequence in parallel; and caches lifted lists across instances. It wrote the same quadruple sets as wc_lift at 45 (3,501 instances), 57 (58,408) and 63 (480,403), and is about sixteen times faster per instance at 65. An order-65 replay may split its instance lines between the two lifters; the report's command records the split.

`williamson/wil_hkt.c` (engine id williamson-hkt-v1, with the shared `kkss/hkt_solve.h`) is the census for prime orders, written 2026-11-14 on the method of Holzmann, Kharaghani and Tayfeh-Rezaie (2008): A runs over the symmetric sequences that are least in their orbit under the multipliers of Z_n (the multipliers act on all four sequences at once, so one sequence can be taken canonical), B over the symmetric sequences of its row sum under the PSD ceiling, each pair passing the pointwise ceiling PSD_A + PSD_B <= 4n is handed to the mod-4 linearisation of the remaining PAF equations, which the shared solver reduces in two GF(2) stages to a handful of candidates for C and D, and every candidate is checked exactly. It is nw_c.c of vxvware/saw-best-matrix with both enumerated rows symmetric. Its class sets equal the direct census at every odd order from 3 to 43 and the published classification at 25, 35, 39, 41, 43 and 47 (empty); at 47 it takes 23 s on four threads where the pair-table census took 6829 s on sixteen. Both solver paths stop at H = 32 (orders to 63). `williamson/wil_hkt2.c` (engine id williamson-hkt-v2, same solver header) is the 2026-11-14 revision of the per-pair solve: a table over the 2^H sign patterns holds the left null vectors of the stage-2 system, so a pair whose folded right-hand side fails the parity test is dropped before any elimination (half of all pairs at the prime orders 47 to 61, where every pattern has corank 1); the stage-2 matrix of a passing pair is built by columns in closed form into the eight-lane layout, the elimination skips columns already pivoted in every lane, and the particular solution and null vector are read off for eight lanes at once. Its class sets equal wil_hkt.c at every odd order from 25 to 47 and at 53; on two threads it is 2.2 to 2.4 times faster at 37 to 47 and 3.9 times faster at 53 (930 s against 3587 s), where the original has no cache.

`williamson/wil_hkt3.c` with `kkss/hkt_solve64.h` (engine id williamson-hkt-v3) is wil_hkt2.c on a widened copy of the solver, written 2026-11-14 so that orders above 63 can run: built with -DHKT_WIDE, the H-bit words become 64-bit and the n-bit row words 128-bit, so the order limit moves to 127; the batched elimination has four lanes instead of eight; and the B record no longer stores each row's PAF, which the exact check recomputes from the code, so a B row is 32 bytes instead of 73 (3.1 GB instead of 7.2 GB for the largest group at 67). The consistency table is off above H = 30. Nothing in the method changed. Its class sets and pair counts equal wil_hkt2.c's at every odd order from 25 to 53, and the rebuilt source reproduces the 43, 45 and 47 rows byte for byte. Every closed form is checked at startup against the reference computation for the order in hand, including the 128-bit high lane at 67 and 69, but no order above 63 had run end to end when it was pinned.

`kkss/gmat_c.c` with `kkss/hkt_solve.h` (engine id kkss-census-c-v1) is nearwill/gmat_c.c of vxvware/saw-best-matrix at 4a25e3c, the engine that produced every G-matrix census on the board: A and B run over skew sequences, A least in its orbit under the multipliers, B indexed by quantised PSD levels, and the symmetric C and D are solved from the mod-4 linearisation of the PAF equations. The levels (NLEV) and the cache-free solve are build-time choices that only coarsen the pruning or change the solve path, never the result; a replay report records its build command. `kkss/gmat_c_v2.c` (engine id kkss-census-c-v2, same solver header) is the 2026-11-14 revision: a consistency table of the left null vectors of the stage-2 system rejects most survivors by parity before any elimination, the stage-2 matrix is built by columns in closed form straight into the eight-lane layout, the AND chain runs four words per vector register, and the memory layout is tightened. It emits the same row sets as the stored censuses at 37, 41, 43 and 45 and is 2.2 to 2.7 times faster there on four threads.

`general/gencensus.c` (engine ids general-census-c-v1 and, built with -DGEN_SKEW, skew-sds-census-c-v1) is the exact census of the two unconstrained tracks at odd orders 3 to 13, written 2026-11-14. It groups the ±1 sequences with x_0 = +1 by periodic autocorrelation vector, keeping one sequence per orbit under shifts, reversal and negation (the least skew member when the orbit has one), meets in the middle on the vectors of pairs partitioned by a^2 + b^2, keeps the index 4-tuples that are least under the simultaneous multipliers, and emits the product of the four sequence lists; it also reports the exact class count by Burnside's lemma, which the verifier compares against the classes it derives from the lines. Both engines are replayed under equiv-v2, the shift quotient of notes/mathematical-contract.md, because under equiv-v1 the unconstrained tracks have 514 and 152 classes already at n=7 and over ten million at n=13. Before it was pinned, its equiv-v2 class sets were checked equal to those of the exhaustive equiv-v1 enumeration of the same engine without the shift reduction at orders 3 to 13 (brute force over every quadruple at 3 and 5, over 2^24 raw quadruples at 7), and the module's class count equalled the Burnside count at every order.

`kkss/gmat_c_v3.c` (engine id kkss-census-c-v3, same solver header) is the 2026-11-14 revision of gmat_c_v2.c: A and B are both skew with row sum 1, so (A, B, C, D) and (B, A, C, D) are one class and only pairs with the orbit minimum of B at least the code of A are scanned, which halves the pairs; all row-sum decompositions share one pass over the pairs; the stage-2 right-hand side is folded from two words of A and two of B as in wil_hkt2.c, so the consistency test reads one 16-byte B record; the elimination skips pivoted columns and the tail is lane-vectorised; the nullity cap is raised to 12 and every solve dropped by it is counted and reported, so a census is exhaustive only when the report's count is zero. Its environment variables (GMAT_, NW_ prefixes) only tune performance or partition the B table across runs (GMAT_BPIECES, GMAT_BPIECE, whose outputs are concatenated). Its equiv-v1 class sets equal gmat_c_v2's at every odd order from 25 to 53 and the registered v1 census at 55 (three classes, 2072 s on eight threads beside another job against 11,088 s for v2 on four); on two threads it is 2.7 to 6.3 times faster than v2 at 41 to 51.

`good_gpu/` is the Gaussian v4 snapshot archived with the n=67 campaign in
vxvware/good-matrix-campaign, commit
018ef2d7754017eca1f9f22c8ddd84b9b6cf5581. This is important: the n=67 run used
Gaussian v4, since the v5 CRT implementation could not represent its degree-33
field. Historical good-65 passes used multiple versions. Replaying with this
pinned engine is a new check, not proof of the exact original binary.

`cyclotomic_sweep.py` is a new exact-PAF join implementing the same precisely
stated same-subgroup search scope. The archived p=73 candidates are independently
verified on import; an old sweep result is never used as an exhaustive census.

Sage imports contain numerical rows extracted from a pinned literal table,
not executed Sage code. The source SHA and original Hall/London/Szekeres
references are retained on each imported entry.

`best/best_hkt.c` (engine id best-census-hkt-v1) is a 2026-11-17 census of circulant best matrices by the mod-4 linearisation used in the G-matrix and good-matrix engines, rederived for two unknown skew rows: it enumerates the symmetric row D and one skew row A and solves for the skew rows B and C. Its header states the exhaustiveness argument and the nine symmetry reductions. The only pruning that can drop a solution is a cap of 20 on the stage-2 nullity; every system dropped by it is counted and reported, so a census is exhaustive only when the report's count is zero. Its environment variables (BEST_ prefix) tune performance or switch off optional reductions; BEST_PROBE and BEST_PLANT select sampling and recovery modes that are not censuses. Its equiv-v1 class sets equal the board's best censuses at 3, 7, 13, 21, 31 and 43 (1, 2, 2, 7, 2 and 5 classes), its raw output equals a from-the-definition enumeration at 3, 7 and 13, and on two threads it takes 1.8 s at 43 against 80 s for census.c, which cannot run 57.

`good/good_hkt.c` (engine id good-hkt-v1, built with `-I research/starter/kkss` for hkt_solve.h) is a census of circulant good matrices that shares no search code with good_c.c: it enumerates the skew row A up to multipliers and one symmetric row B and solves for the other two symmetric rows by the mod-4 linearisation of wil_hkt2.c, with a null-vector consistency table and an eight-lane AVX2 elimination. It covers odd orders 3 to 63. The solver's nullity cap is counted and reported, so a census is exhaustive only when that count is zero. Its GOOD_ environment variables tune speed and memory only. Its equiv-v1 class sets equal good_c.c's at every odd order from 7 to 47 (0 capped throughout), and on two threads it is 6.6 to 9.4 times faster than good_c.c at 43, 45 and 47.

`best/best_hkt2.c` (engine id best-census-hkt-v2) is the 2026-11-18 revision of best_hkt.c with the same nine reductions and a faster solve: stage tables in place of 128-bit row arithmetic, an elimination batched one system per AVX2 lane, two unknowns fixed in advance, a consistency table split into classes of patterns so that it fits at order 73, and a 256-level per-pair spectrum test. `BEST_CLASS` and `BEST_PIECE` run one unit of work per invocation; the units partition the (A, D) pairs, and `best/run_units.sh` drives them with restart markers and sums the capped counts. Its sorted output is identical to best_hkt.c's at 3, 7, 13, 21, 31 and 43 in both builds, and a full census at 57 through the driver (1129 s on two threads against 4027 s) wrote the same 9 rows as the registered best-census-hkt-v1 run, with no capped systems. The `-DWIDE` build needed above 63 has been exercised only at 3 to 43 and by sampling probes at 73.

`skew/pool_plan.py`, `skew/pool_gen.c` and `skew/pool_join.c` are a search for quadruples with one skew block, written for this board on 2026-11-18 and 2026-11-19. It is a heuristic, not a census: an empty result proves nothing. The skew block A is fixed in advance, constant on the orbits of a multiplier subgroup H of odd order, so its periodic autocorrelation takes few values and is invariant under H. pool_plan.py fixes, for each lag and each of the three free blocks, a window of three or four consecutive admissible autocorrelation values, placed so that in-window triples can cancel the autocorrelation of A at that lag; the windows inherit the invariance under H. pool_gen.c samples sequences of a given row sum whose autocorrelation lies in its block's window at every lag, by annealing with swaps, and rechecks each from the row before writing it; every image of such a sequence under H is another one. pool_join.c expands the three samples over H, keeps one representative per orbit in one of them, and finds every triple that cancels A at all lags by a trie join on the packed autocorrelation vectors, so the join is exact for the samples it is given and each orbit of solutions appears once. Each solution is rechecked from its rows at every lag before it is printed, and the board verifies it again on submission. The solution count predicted by the Gaussian model in pool_plan.py was low by a factor of 16 to 52 in the runs at 53, 61, 67 and 71 (|H| = 13, 15, 33, 35), which found 15, 11, 9 and 1 orbits of solutions.

`skew/pool_gen_orbit.c` and `skew/orbit_rows.py`, written 2026-11-20, restrict the same search to quadruples in which all four blocks are constant on the orbits of H. pool_gen_orbit.c reads the same plan and writes the same format as pool_gen.c, but its annealer flips whole orbits, so it samples only orbit-constant sequences, and pool_join.c joins its pools unchanged. Such a block has row sum plus or minus one plus a signed sum of orbit sizes, so most decompositions of 4n are excluded; orbit_rows.py lists the row-sum triples that remain, and the plan is made with one of them in ROWS. The pools are small (a few thousand sequences at |H| = 3 and n near 100) and the generator soon repeats itself, so a run is close to exhaustive for the one skew block and row-sum triple of its plan and says nothing about the others. With |H| = 3 it found 350 orbits of solutions at n=73 in 20 seconds on two threads and one at n=97 in 90 seconds, against about 3e7 annealing moves per solution at 73 for a direct annealer in the same orbit space. Since 2026-11-20 the generator keeps one sign per orbit and writes each autocorrelation as a quadratic form in those signs, with the change from every single flip kept up to date; the chain is the one the first version ran (one thread writes the same file, checked at n=105 and n=205) and it runs five times faster at n=105 with |H| = 3 and eight times faster at n=205 with |H| = 5. `skew/orbit_search.c`, written 2026-11-17, is the exhaustive search of the same orbit space for the groups where it is small: it enumerates every skew orbit-constant A and every orbit-constant B, C, D, drops blocks whose row sum or power spectrum rules them out, and meets the pairs (A, B) against the pairs (C, D) on a hash of the autocorrelation vector, rechecking every match at all lags. Its RESULT line reports the solution count, whether it stopped early at the cap, and how many row-sum decompositions it skipped for exceeding the pair budget; only a run with neither is a complete search of that group's orbit space. It returns exactly the nine board quadruples at n=73 among its 1232 solutions for |H| = 9, and 5,733,738 solutions at n=127 for |H| = 7. `skew/pool_join_orbit.c`, written 2026-11-20, is pool_join.c for orbit-constant pools: the autocorrelation of an orbit-constant row is constant on the classes of lags under H and -1, so its key takes one lag per class with four bits each, which admits windows of up to 16 values, lengths up to 255 and at most 24 classes; rows are not expanded over H, and rows that are not orbit-constant are skipped. It returns the same solutions as pool_join.c on the orbit-constant pools at 73 and 97 (350 and 52). Wide windows matter when H is larger: at n=121 with |H| = 5 the pools at width 4 are nearly empty, width 12 gave 2 solutions and width 16 gave 63 from pools of 22,000 to 31,000 rows, which at 24 orbits is close to an exact search for the one skew block of the plan.

`compress/pc_compress.c` and `compress/pc_lift.c` (engine ids williamson-compress-v3, good-compress-v1, kkss-compress-v1 and best-compress-v1, one per stratum, all on the same two sources) are a compression census for the four symmetric families, written 2026-11-23. pc_compress takes the block pattern (ssss, ksss, kkss or kkks, skew blocks first), enumerates every skew row and every symmetric row whose row sum fits, keeps those under the PSD ceiling, compresses them by d and matches the realized compressions into instances on the length-m PAF condition, one instance per orbit of the units of Z_m; pc_lift is wc_lift5.c extended to skew blocks. Above wc_compress.c and wc_lift5.c it adds: rows in 128-bit words and codes in 63-bit words, so the compression stage runs to n = 125 and a sparse count table replaces the dense one when the compression keys outgrow it; a match that hashes the pair PAF vector, splits the pair table into as many passes as the PC_MEM_MB budget needs, and can run as K independent shards; a stage-1 split of the code range into pieces whose list files are summed by a merge mode that refuses pieces that do not tile the range; a skip of left pairs whose first block is not least in its multiplier orbit, which the kept instance always is; and a lifter build with -DPC_WIDE whose PAF and PSD vectors have 64 16-bit lanes, for n up to 127 (the default build stores PAF values as bytes, covers n up to 65 and aborts if a kept row has a PAF value outside [-63, 63]). Every lifted quadruple is rechecked from its rows before it is written. `compress/run_census.sh` drives a census as restartable units (enum pieces, merge, match shards, lift line ranges) with a STOP file and per-unit logs. The instance counts by 3 for good matrices equal Table 1 of bright2019good in baselines/references.bib at every order it lists from 9 to 63 (808,642 at 63, compressed in 10 minutes on four threads within 1.1 GB through two match shards), and by 5 at 65 the count is 67,437. Full censuses on four threads gave the board's equiv-v1 class sets for good matrices at 45 (d = 3 and 5), 51 and 57, G-matrices at 45, 49 (d = 7), 51, 55 (d = 5) and 57, best matrices at 57 and Williamson matrices at 51; good 57 took 5 minutes and G 55 took 38. The narrow and wide lifters wrote the same rows at every one of these orders where both were run (45, 51 and best 57). tests/test_compress_engine.py checks the instance counts to 45, the two lifters against each other, and the classes against the board's censuses at 21 to 39 in all four patterns.
